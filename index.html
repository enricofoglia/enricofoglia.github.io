About

Ciao, thank you for passing by! I'm Enrico, and I am a PhD candidate at ISAE-Supaéro (France) and Université de Sherbrooke (Canada), under the supervision of Michael Bauerheim, Thierry Jardin and Stéphane Moreau.

I work on understanding an modelling pressure fields under turbulent boundary layers. I am developing an approach based on large deviations theory—the mathematics of rare events—to generate statistically homogeneous fields subject to some prior information about their bulk behavior.

I am mainly focused on aeroacoustics and energy-based models, but I am more broadely interested in statistical mechanics, turbulence, large deviations theory, random processes. Do not hesitate to contact me about any of these topics!

Publications

Analysis of Airfoil Self-Noise Sources Using Wavelet-Based Denoising

E. Foglia, J. Rendon, S. Moreau, M. Bauerheim, T. Jardin

Predicting trailing-edge noise requires one to be able to measure the turbulent pressure fluctuations near the trailing edge. However, experimental measurements of this crucial quantity are polluted by the acoustics. In this paper, we propose that the Coherent Vortex Extraction algorithm, which has been successfully used in many similar scenarios, can be used to separate the hydrodynamic and the acoustic components of experimentally measured pressure signals.

Empirical uncertainty quantification in deep learning: Applications to fluid dynamics

E. Foglia, B. Bobbia, M. Bauerheim, T. Jardin, S. Moreau, P. Fua

With deep learning models entering more and more into fields of applications like science and engineering, practitioners often push back because of the lack of guarantees in the extrapolation regime, i.e. when the model is asked to make predictions outside the boundaries of its training dataset. In this paper, we review some of the leading techniques to predict the model's internal uncertainty, often called epistemic, and we apply them to two datasets of interest for the fluid dynamics community.

Towards the optimization of propeller tonal noise in the frequency domain using the discrete adjoint method

E. Foglia, M. Daroukh, M. Buszyk, I. Salah el-Din, and S. Moreau

Numerically optimizing rotors targeting their noise emission is challenging because computing the gradient can be extremely expensive. By leveraging the adjoint of the linearized Navier–Stokes equations, however, it is possible to compute the sensitivity of an objective function to an arbitrary number of parameters with a single computation. In this paper, we derive analytically the linearization of the acoustic pressure generated by a rotating blade, so that it can be used in any adjoint solver to take acoustics into account.
